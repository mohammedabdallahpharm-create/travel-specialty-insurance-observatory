# UAE Travel & Pet Insurance — Claims Operations Research Dossier

**Research date:** 11 November 2026  
**Market:** United Arab Emirates  
**Scope:** Travel Insurance (including Baggage Protection) and companion-animal Pet Insurance  
**Research priority:** Claims Operations, role allocation, evidence requirements, decision authority and complaints  
**Status:** Initial desk research; not a comprehensive market census, legal opinion, or audit of actual insurer operations.

---

## 1. Executive findings

The public evidence reviewed supports several distinct operating models rather than one universal UAE claims model:

1. **AIG Travel Guard:** the policy identifies American Home Assurance Company (Dubai Branch) as insurer/underwriter and provides a direct claims department and portal. The policy language assigns claim notification, evidence requests and indemnity payments to the insurer ("We"). Public material does not identify a separate external TPA for this product.
2. **Allianz Travel UAE:** Alliance Insurance Company P.S.C. is identified as underwriter; NEXtCARE is identified as service provider. The public claims journey uses NEXtCARE email addresses and a dedicated claims assessor. The available public pages do not disclose the full delegation of authority or establish whether NEXtCARE can make final coverage decisions, approve reserves or authorise payments.
3. **ADNIC Travel:** ADNIC publishes a direct customer claims route and a documented sequence of incident reporting, document submission, assessment and claim-reference tracking. The assistance company is referred to as the company named in the policy schedule/certificate, not named in the reviewed claims page.
4. **MetLife Pet:** the policy wording identifies American Life Insurance Company (MetLife) as the insurer. MetLife names MedNet as its claims partner and uses MedNet's online reimbursement submission route. The public page says MetLife pays approved claims. This proves a claims submission partnership, not MedNet's final adjudication authority.
5. **MooPet:** policies issued before 1 June 2026 were underwritten by Alliance Insurance; policies issued on or after that date are underwritten by Qatar Insurance Company (QIC). The public process uses a form completed by the customer and veterinarian and an email address at kainosint.com. The public page does not identify Kainos' legal entity, contractual role or delegated authority.

**Central conclusion:** public-facing claims channels reveal who receives claims and supports the customer, but usually do not disclose the actual claims authority matrix. We can identify some insurer-level decision rights from policy wording, but cannot safely attribute delegated approval or rejection authority to TPAs, assistance providers or claims administrators without contractual evidence.

## 2. Evidence and source limitations

### Evidence classification used

- **Verified:** directly stated in a primary source for the specific claim.
- **Partially Verified:** the source establishes part of a role or process, but not the complete responsibility.
- **Unverified:** a plausible lead that lacks adequate corroboration.
- **Hypothesis:** an interpretation to be tested.
- **Historical:** evidence is valid for an earlier policy/product period and may not describe current terms.

### Source limitations

- Product marketing pages are useful for current customer journeys, but policy wording and certificates govern cover and contractual obligations.
- A published policy PDF may be an older version than the live product page. Version/date alignment must be checked before treating it as current.
- A named claims partner or portal proves a customer-facing service role; it does not by itself prove authority to accept, decline, settle or pay claims.
- Public information rarely exposes outsourcing contracts, delegated authority limits, audit rights, service-level agreements or internal escalation matrices.
- We have not interviewed the companies, inspected actual claim files, or independently tested any claims portal.

## 4. Travel Insurance — operating models

### 4.1 AIG Travel Guard

**Product / insurer**
- AIG states that Travel Guard is underwritten by American Home Assurance Company (Dubai Branch).
- The policy document identifies the Dubai Branch, its CBUAE authorisation and its claims department.

**Customer claims process**
- Customer notifies the insurer by telephone, email or online claims portal.
- The policy requires written notice within 90 days of the incident; it also sets a subsequent submission period. The precise interaction between these two periods should be interpreted from the full policy and certificate for an actual claim.
- The insurer furnishes claim forms/instructions and may request additional evidence.
- Policy wording states that indemnity payments are made by the insurer to the insured/appropriate party.
- AIG's current product page states that claims are processed within seven working days after all required documents are submitted.

**Evidence requirements and controls**
- AIG policy wording requires evidence of loss/expenses and may require further information.
- Police documentation is required for claims arising from criminal incidents.
- The policy includes fraud provisions allowing benefits to be forfeited and further action to be taken where dishonesty or exaggeration is established.
- The insurer reserves rights to obtain medical records and require medical examination in relevant cases.
- Baggage cover requires proof and official reporting in specified circumstances; unattended baggage and some carrier-custody situations are restricted.

**Decision authority**
- **Verified at insurer-contract level:** the policy consistently defines "We" as the insurer and assigns claim instructions, evidence requirements and payments to it.
- **Not established:** whether any third-party service provider performs intake, document indexing, medical review or preliminary assessment behind the scenes.
- **Research decision:** insurer-led public claims model; do not label it fully in-house until operational sourcing confirms that.

### 4.2 Allianz Travel UAE

**Product / role allocation**
- Allianz Travel UAE identifies Alliance Insurance Company P.S.C. as underwriter.
- It identifies NEXtCARE as service provider.
- Customer-facing claims and emergency contact channels use NEXtCARE domains and contact details.

**Customer claims process**
- Medical emergencies: 24/7 contact with the emergency assistance team; the published guidance says the team coordinates care and may arrange direct billing in participating/network hospitals.
- Non-emergency reimbursement: online claims portal, telephone, email or WhatsApp.
- A dedicated claims assessor reviews the case and contacts the claimant if additional information is needed.
- Approved claims are reimbursed according to policy terms.
- For baggage incidents, the published process requires an airline Property Irregularity Report (PIR) for airline-related loss/damage, proof of ownership, and police report for theft. It also gives product-specific reporting deadlines.
- For trip cancellation/interruption, it requires booking and cancellation evidence and proof of the covered reason.
- For medical claims, it asks for invoices, treatment reports, prescriptions and proof of travel.

**Decision authority**
- **Verified:** Alliance is the underwriter; NEXtCARE is the service provider; the dedicated assessor reviews claims.
- **Not established:** whether NEXtCARE has delegated authority to approve/decline claims or merely assesses and recommends decisions for Alliance.
- **Not established:** who has final authority for exceptional medical cases, high-value claims, fraud referrals, settlement overrides and appeals.
- **Research priority:** obtain the policy's claims procedure and complaint clauses, then seek direct confirmation of NEXtCARE's delegated authority.

### 4.3 ADNIC Travel

**Published customer journey**
1. Customer reports the incident and submits supporting documents.
2. ADNIC records incident details and assesses the case.
3. ADNIC provides next-step guidance and issues an SMS claim reference after the claim is opened.
4. The claimant can track progress using the reference.

**Visible evidence requirements**
- Baggage/flight claims: policy number, flight details, estimated value of lost items and any airline compensation.
- Travel document claims: details of the lost document, time/date, reporting authority and replacement cost.
- Accident/medical claims: incident details, police report for accidents, doctor/hospital details for illness.
- Emergency assistance: claimant is directed to the assistance company named in the schedule/certificate.

**Decision authority**
- **Verified:** ADNIC publicly describes itself as recording and assessing the travel claim.
- **Not established:** whether an external administrator or assistance company handles some stages.
- **Not established:** identity of assistance company across all ADNIC travel products; the schedule/certificate is product-specific.

### 4.4 Baggage Protection — analytical subdomain

Baggage claims are not simply "lost property reimbursement". The reviewed AIG and Allianz evidence shows a chain of evidence and possible recovery:

- Carrier/airline report (PIR) where loss or damage occurred in airline custody.
- Police report for theft.
- Proof of ownership and value: receipts, bank statements, warranty cards or photographs.
- Repair estimate and retention of damaged property where damage is repairable.
- Evidence of carrier compensation or other insurance payment.
- Time-bound notice/submission requirements.

**Operational implications**
- Claims assessors must distinguish loss, delay, damage and theft.
- The airline/handling agent is a source of evidence and potentially another compensating party, not automatically the insurer's claims administrator.
- Other insurance and carrier payments can affect the insured loss and recoverable amount.
- Proof-of-ownership gaps, missing PIRs and late police reports are predictable friction points.
- Subrogation and recovery rights may apply depending on policy terms.

**Research gap:** compare exact policy wording across AIG, Allianz and ADNIC before creating a common UAE baggage control framework. Do not treat Allianz's product-specific 21-day instruction as a market-wide rule.

## 5. Pet Insurance — operating models

### 5.1 MetLife Pet Insurance

**Insurer identity**
- The reviewed UAE policy wording names American Life Insurance Company (MetLife) as the company providing insurance.
- The policy is subject to UAE law and identifies the UAE branch.
- The live product page describes cover for dogs and cats, annual limits and reimbursement levels.

**Customer claims process**
1. Customer takes the pet to a registered veterinary clinic and pays the bill.
2. Customer submits an itemised veterinary invoice and receipt through MedNet's online reimbursement route.
3. MetLife confirms receipt and reviews the claim.
4. MetLife states that approved claims are reimbursed to the policyholder's bank account within seven days, subject to complete documentation.

**Policy controls visible in the wording**
- 30-day waiting period for illness; accident cover is described as commencing from the policy start in current product information.
- Pre-existing conditions and congenital/hereditary conditions are excluded in the reviewed policy wording.
- Treatment must be medically necessary and supported by evidence.
- Preventive care, grooming, elective/cosmetic procedures and other specified costs are excluded unless an endorsement changes the position.
- A UAE-licensed veterinarian is required in the policy wording.
- Deductible, covered percentage and maximum benefit shape the amount payable.
- The policy wording gives the insurer a right to obtain an independent expert opinion in a dispute about the correctness or appropriateness of a diagnosis.

**Important version discrepancy**
- The current MetLife product page says customers may use any registered veterinary clinic in the UAE and that there is no fixed vet network.
- The reviewed policy PDF defines a "Medical Network" as clinics/providers approved and published by MetLife, and the PDF includes wording that appears to restrict treatment outside that network.
- The PDF is an older published version. We cannot assume the current certificate/wording has the same network restriction or that the live page supersedes all policy terms.
- **Action:** obtain the current policy wording and certificate applicable to a new policy before making a definitive network statement.

**Decision authority**
- **Verified:** MedNet is the claims submission partner/channel; MetLife is the insurer and states that it pays approved claims.
- **Partially verified:** the policy grants the insurer a right to call for an independent expert opinion in diagnosis disputes.
- **Not established:** whether MedNet performs document checks only, medical/clinical review, coverage recommendations or delegated approval within financial limits.
- **Not established:** whether MedNet can reject claims or issue a binding decision on MetLife's behalf.

### 5.2 MooPet (MooCover)

**Underwriting transition**
- MooPet states that policies issued before 1 June 2026 were underwritten by Alliance Insurance.
- Policies issued on or after 1 June 2026 are underwritten by Qatar Insurance Company (QIC).
- The customer complaint channel changes accordingly: Alliance for the earlier cohort, QIC for the later cohort.

**Published claims process**
1. Customer downloads and completes the claim form.
2. A section of the form is completed by the veterinarian.
3. Customer emails the completed form and supporting information to claims.moopet@kainosint.com.
4. MooPet says covered claims are typically settled within ten business days after all information is received.
5. If more details are required, MooPet says it will contact the customer within five business days.

**Decision authority**
- **Verified:** underwriter identity depends on policy issue date.
- **Verified:** Kainos-branded email domain is the published claim-submission route.
- **Not established:** the legal identity of the entity behind the Kainos email, its role, its contract with MooPet/QIC, or its authority to approve/decline/settle claims.
- **Not established:** whether QIC handles claims directly or uses a delegated administrator.
- **Action:** identify the legal company behind Kainos International and search for policy wording, claims forms, privacy notice and corporate disclosures. Do not add Kainos to the verified entity registry until the role is corroborated.

## 7. Regulatory and complaint-resolution layer

### CBUAE

The Central Bank of the UAE regulates and supervises the insurance sector. It took over the former Insurance Authority's regulatory, supervisory, licensing and enforcement functions following the 2020 merger. This establishes the regulatory perimeter for licensed insurers; it does not make CBUAE the operational handler of an individual travel or pet claim.

### Sanadak

Sanadak is the independent ombudsman unit for eligible complaints involving CBUAE-licensed insurers and financial institutions.

The current Sanadak eligibility guidance says a complainant should:
- first submit an official complaint to the insurer;
- wait 15 calendar days after submitting it;
- not have the same complaint already before a court or be a duplicate; and
- meet the complainant/product eligibility criteria.

Sanadak may review the complaint and determine whether it is upheld, partially upheld or rejected. The published process may request additional information and can direct corrective action where a complaint is upheld.

**Important discrepancy:** MooPet's page says the customer may refer to Sanadak if dissatisfied or if no response is received within 30 days. Sanadak's current general eligibility page states 15 calendar days. We should use Sanadak's current official eligibility criteria for the general process and flag the MooPet page's 30-day statement as product-page wording requiring clarification, not silently harmonise the two.

### Implication for claims operations

The complaint layer is a separate control and accountability mechanism. A claims administrator may receive or assess a claim, but the consumer complaint route points to the licensed insurer and, if eligible, Sanadak. This makes it important to identify who owns the internal complaint response and who is accountable for correcting an adverse claim decision.

## 8. Key operational patterns and hypotheses

### Verified patterns
1. Travel emergency assistance and non-emergency reimbursement are operationally distinct.
2. Allianz's public model separates underwriting (Alliance) from customer-facing servicing/claims channels (NEXtCARE).
3. AIG's policy gives the insurer a direct claims department and explicit contractual claims/payment responsibilities.
4. ADNIC publishes a direct claims intake and assessment process.
5. MetLife uses a reimbursement model and a named external claims submission partner.
6. MooPet uses a veterinarian-supported claim form and an email submission process; the underwriter changed during 2026.
7. Evidence completeness is a visible control in all the reviewed claim journeys.
8. CBUAE supervision and Sanadak complaint escalation sit outside day-to-day claim intake.

### Hypotheses to test
- H1: Outsourced travel assistance/claims servicing can improve emergency coordination and customer access, while leaving contractual coverage accountability with the insurer.
- H2: Pet reimbursement models may reduce the need for direct billing networks but shift cash-flow and documentation burdens to policyholders.
- H3: The use of external claims partners may create an accountability gap if the customer cannot distinguish the submission channel from the decision owner.
- H4: Clinical documentation quality and policy exclusions are likely to be major sources of friction in pet claims, especially pre-existing conditions, waiting periods and medical necessity.
- H5: Baggage claims require multi-party evidence coordination across the insurer, airline/handler, police and sometimes other insurers.

These are not yet signals or market-wide findings. They are testable propositions derived from the small set of product examples.

## 12. Who owns the claim decision — and who is accountable for the operational outcome?

**Research question:** distinguish (a) the authority to make an individual claim decision, (b) responsibility for performing the outsourced workflow, and (c) the insurer's continuing regulatory and policyholder accountability.

### 12.1 Regulatory baseline — insurer accountability survives outsourcing

The CBUAE's Risk Management and Internal Controls Regulation for Insurance Companies, Article 12 (Outsourcing), states that:
- outsourced material business activities/functions must be subject to oversight, accountability, review and assessment equivalent to activities performed internally;
- the insurance company is fully responsible for risks arising from any process or activity it outsources;
- the Board and Senior Management are ultimately responsible for outsourced functions or activities;
- written contracts must define the parties' rights and obligations; and
- the insurer remains responsible for compliance with applicable CBUAE laws and regulations in outsourced activities.

**Finding — Verified regulatory baseline:** outsourcing does not transfer the insurer's ultimate regulatory accountability for its outsourced insurance operations. This does not, by itself, identify the individual employee or vendor staff member who has delegated authority to approve or decline a particular claim.

The CBUAE's AML/CFT outsourcing guidance separately requires the outsourced entity to operate under the delegating insurer's internal policies and procedures and be subject to the insurer's control and monitoring.

### 12.2 The three-layer accountability model

| Layer | Core question | Accountable party (general UAE insurance baseline) | Evidence needed for a specific product |
|---|---|---|---|
| Policy / coverage obligation | Who owes the insured the benefit under the policy? | Licensed insurer named in the policy, subject to policy terms and applicable law | Current policy wording, certificate and endorsements |
| Delegated claim decision | Who may approve, decline, reserve, settle or override a claim? | The person/entity expressly authorised by the insurer within a documented delegation; cannot be inferred from intake or assessment role | Delegated authority agreement, claims authority matrix, limits, escalation rules and decision letter |
| Operational delivery | Who must process intake, evidence, review, communication and turnaround? | The operational service provider for its contracted tasks; insurer remains responsible for oversight and outsourced risk | Outsourcing/TPA agreement, SLA, process map, audit and quality controls |
| Regulatory governance | Who remains answerable for the outsourced activity at insurer level? | Insurer, with Board and Senior Management ultimately responsible under CBUAE outsourcing requirements | Board-approved outsourcing framework, risk register, oversight reporting and material outsourcing approvals |

**Important:** the vendor can be contractually accountable to the insurer for service failures, SLA breaches or negligent performance. That is a separate relationship from the insurer's continuing obligations to the policyholder and regulator.

### 12.4 Who is accountable when the claim goes wrong?

Use this distinction when analysing a complaint or operational failure:

1. **Wrong coverage decision:** the policyholder's claim is against the insurer named in the policy. If a delegate made the decision under delegated authority, the insurer's recourse against that delegate is a separate contractual matter.
2. **Delay, lost documents or poor communication by a TPA:** the TPA may be contractually accountable to the insurer under the SLA; the insurer remains responsible for oversight and its obligations to the policyholder.
3. **Failure of emergency assistance coordination:** identify the assistance provider's service obligation and the insurer's policy promise; assess the exact contract and circumstances rather than assuming the assistance provider carries the insured risk.
4. **Regulatory control failure:** the insurer remains responsible for outsourced risks; Board and Senior Management retain ultimate responsibility for outsourced functions under CBUAE requirements.
5. **Customer complaint / redress:** follow the insurer's formal complaint process and applicable Sanadak eligibility rules. A service-provider inbox is not automatically the final complaint owner.

A 2026 UAE insurance litigation practice guide also explains that, in delegated-authority disputes, policyholders generally pursue the insurer for obligations under the policy, while the insurer may have a separate contractual claim against an MGA/TPA. Treat this as secondary legal commentary, not a substitute for primary law or case-specific legal advice.

### 12.5 Operational accountability metrics to investigate

For each insurer/TPA arrangement, seek evidence of named ownership and reporting for:

- time from complete documentation to decision;
- percentage of claims returned for missing documents;
- clinical/veterinary review turnaround;
- approval and decline rates by reason;
- appeal and complaint volumes;
- overturned decision rate;
- payment execution time;
- fraud referral and confirmed fraud rates;
- SLA breaches and remediation;
- system outage and business continuity incidents;
- quality assurance sampling and audit findings.

Do not publish target values or performance results unless a source provides them.

### 12.6 Updated research conclusion

**Verified general conclusion:** under CBUAE insurance outsourcing requirements, an insurer cannot outsource away its ultimate regulatory accountability for outsourced operations. The Board and Senior Management remain ultimately responsible for outsourced functions.

**Product-specific conclusion:** we can identify some insurer and service-provider roles, but public sources do not yet establish the full delegated claims decision matrix for NEXtCARE, MedNet or Kainos.

## 13. Does outsourcing claims operations also delegate decision authority?

**Research question:** when an insurer appoints a TPA, does the TPA receive authority to adjudicate claims, or does it only administer the workflow?

### 13.1 Finding: delegation is possible, but not automatic

The appointment of a TPA and the delegation of binding claim-decision authority are separate propositions.

A TPA may be contracted to:
- receive and register claims;
- check documents and eligibility data;
- apply benefit rules and exclusions;
- conduct medical/veterinary or technical review;
- recommend an outcome;
- issue an approval or rejection within delegated limits;
- authorise or execute payment;
- manage complaints and appeals.

The contract may allocate only some of these functions. A submission portal, claims email address, named assessor, or description such as "claims management" does not, by itself, prove every authority above.

### 13.2 Regulatory distinction across UAE insurance segments

**CBUAE-regulated insurance outsourcing — general insurer baseline**

The CBUAE Risk Management and Internal Controls Regulation and Standards require board-approved outsourcing governance, written contracts, risk assessment, monitoring, oversight and access to information. The insurer remains fully responsible for risks arising from outsourced processes, and the Board and Senior Management remain ultimately responsible for outsourced functions. The CBUAE outsourcing standards also state that the Central Bank will generally not permit outsourcing of core insurance activities and key management/control functions, subject to assessment of individual requests.

Source: https://rulebook.centralbank.ae/en/rulebook/12-outsourcing
Source: https://rulebook.centralbank.ae/en/rulebook/risk-management-and-internal-controls-regulation-insurance-companies

**Dubai health insurance — specific Claim Management Company framework**

Dubai Administrative Resolution No. 78 of 2022 is specifically about health insurance in the Emirate of Dubai. It provides a more explicit arrangement:
- Article 1 defines a Claim Management Company as an establishment licensed to conduct insurance claim settlement and authorised by DHIC for Dubai health insurance.
- Article 3(6) requires an insurer to fulfil its responsibilities even when it contracts a Claim Management Company to manage claims.
- Article 8(4) says the Claim Management Company assumes joint liability with the contracted Insurance Company for managing insurance claims.
- Article 14 requires the insurer–Claim Management Company contract to specify, among other things, records and insurer access, claims settlement periods, statistical reporting, complaint-handling responsibility, payment obligations, service levels and performance indicators.

Source: https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2022/Administrative%20Resolution%20No.%20(78)%20of%202022%20Issuing%20the%20Implementing%20Bylaw%20of%20Law%20No.%20(11)%20of%202013.html

A DHA/DHIC circular issued 20 May 2026 also defines a Dubai health insurance TPA as performing claims management and claims review including adjudication, as well as management of the health insurance claims settlement cycle. The circular is directed to Dubai's health insurance system and must not be treated as a travel or pet insurance rule.

Source: https://www.isahd.ae/content/docs/GC%2005-2026_Broker%20Guidelines%20Circular.pdf

**Scope limitation:** the Dubai health-insurance provisions above cannot be automatically extended to Travel Insurance or Pet Insurance. Those domains require product-specific regulatory classification, the applicable policy and the actual outsourcing/delegation agreement. The CBUAE's general outsourcing rules also require attention to whether the outsourced task is considered a core insurance activity.

### 13.4 How an insurer retains control when decision authority is delegated

A defensible control model should include the following contractual and operational mechanisms. These are research criteria and good-practice controls, not assertions that every sampled company currently implements them.

1. **Written Delegated Authority Matrix**
   - claim types and policy sections in scope;
   - approval, decline, settlement and payment permissions;
   - monetary limits and aggregate exposure limits;
   - exclusions or decisions reserved to the insurer;
   - authority to waive conditions or make ex-gratia payments;
   - referral triggers for complex, disputed, high-value or suspicious claims.

2. **Rules and policy-version control**
   - insurer-approved policy wording and rules engine configuration;
   - effective dates and version history;
   - controlled updates and testing;
   - no unilateral TPA changes to coverage rules.

3. **Human escalation and override**
   - mandatory referral for exceptions and conflicts;
   - insurer access to clinical, veterinary, legal and fraud specialists;
   - documented override and appeal rights;
   - no unreviewed automation for cases outside delegated limits.

4. **File, data and audit rights**
   - insurer access to complete claim files, evidence, decision rationale and audit trail;
   - quality sampling and independent file reviews;
   - regulator access where required;
   - retention, portability and return of data at contract termination.

5. **Performance and outcome monitoring**
   - acknowledgement and end-to-end turnaround;
   - complete-file-to-decision time;
   - missing-document rates;
   - approval/decline rates by reason and product;
   - appeal volume and overturn rate;
   - complaint rates and remediation time;
   - payment accuracy, leakage and duplicate/fraud detection;
   - SLA breaches, root-cause analysis and corrective actions.

6. **Governance and intervention**
   - named insurer owner for the outsourced function;
   - regular operational and risk committees;
   - escalation to senior management and the Board for material failures;
   - remediation plans, step-in rights, suspension and termination rights;
   - business continuity and exit plans.

### 13.5 The practical test: delegated decision vs retained control

For any sampled insurer–TPA relationship, the Observatory should seek documentary answers to these questions:

- Can the TPA approve a claim without insurer sign-off?
- Can it decline a claim without insurer sign-off?
- What claim value and risk categories are within its authority?
- Who approves exceptions, ex-gratia payments and policy-condition waivers?
- Who owns clinical/veterinary disputes and fraud referrals?
- Who signs the decision letter and whose name appears on it?
- Who can reverse or override the TPA's decision?
- Does the insurer receive all claim files and decision logs in real time?
- What happens if SLA or decision-quality thresholds are breached?
- Who is responsible for responding to the policyholder and regulator?

### 13.6 Updated conclusion

**General answer:** an insurer may delegate defined claims decision-making authority to a TPA where the applicable regulatory framework and contract permit it. Delegation is not implied merely by outsourcing claims operations or appointing a TPA.

**Control answer:** the insurer retains control through a documented authority matrix, policy/rules governance, access to files and data, monitoring, audit, escalation, override, and Board/Senior Management oversight. Under CBUAE insurance outsourcing requirements, the insurer's ultimate regulatory accountability for outsourced risks remains.

**Dubai health-insurance nuance:** the Dubai framework expressly recognises Claim Management Companies, claims adjudication and joint liability with the insurer for claims management. This is a segment-specific legal model, not a blanket rule for travel or pet insurance.

**Sample-level conclusion:** NEXtCARE's claims management role is verified; MedNet's participation in adjudication is now evidenced by its live reimbursement form; neither source establishes the full final decision authority matrix for the sampled Travel/Pet products. Kainos remains unverified beyond the published intake email route.

**Research decision:** Continue Research. The next evidence target is the actual insurer–TPA delegation agreement, or a public policy clause explicitly allocating approval, rejection, settlement and escalation powers.

## 14. AI in Claims Operations — UAE evidence and decision authority

**Research question:** when AI is introduced into insurance operations, does it process claims, support human decisions, or exercise authority to approve, decline or settle claims?

**Research date:** 11 November 2026  
**Scope:** UAE insurance market; priority domains remain Travel and Pet. Evidence from other insurance lines is used only as cross-market operational context and is not treated as proof of Travel/Pet deployment.

### 14.1 Initial finding

The public evidence confirms that AI-supported operational automation is already being used by UAE insurers. However, the strongest public examples identified in this research concern document processing and customer case management, not autonomous final adjudication of Travel or Pet claims.

The evidence therefore supports a distinction between:
1. **Process automation:** intake, document reading, data extraction, classification and routing.
2. **Decision support:** summarising evidence, identifying exceptions and recommending next actions.
3. **Delegated automated decision:** approving, declining or settling a claim within a defined authority.
4. **Autonomous high-impact decision:** a system makes a material insurance-claim determination without meaningful human involvement.

The first two are evidenced in UAE insurer announcements reviewed here. The third and fourth have not been established for the sampled UAE Travel or Pet products.

### 14.2 UAE deployment evidence

- AI-supported document automation was deployed in a UAE insurer's claims-registration process.
- The disclosed use case concerns document/data capture and process efficiency.
- Human staff remained involved in exception handling.

**What it does not verify**
- Autonomous coverage decisions.
- AI authority to approve, decline, settle or pay claims.
- Deployment in Travel or Pet Insurance.

### 14.3 CBUAE guidance — human oversight and accountability

The CBUAE Guidance Note on Consumer Protection and the Responsible Adoption and Use of AI and Machine Learning by Licensed Financial Institutions in the UAE applies to licensed financial institutions, including insurance providers. It was issued in February 2026.

The guidance defines a **High-impact decision** as a determination using AI that materially affects a customer's access to financial products or services, giving an insurance claim as an example.

The guidance identifies three human-oversight models:

1. **Human-in-the-loop:** AI provides recommendations, but a human decision-maker retains full authority to approve or reject the outcome.
2. **Human-on-the-loop:** AI works autonomously for routine tasks, while a human monitors outcomes and can intervene where necessary.
3. **Human-out-of-the-loop:** AI operates without direct human involvement; this should only be used for low-risk, non-material processes with appropriate controls.

The level of human involvement should be proportionate to the risks posed to consumers. Consumers should be able to request human review or explanation of AI-generated decisions and challenge decisions or correct inaccurate data that affects them.

The guidance also states that:
- Boards and Senior Management are responsible and accountable for AI systems and outcomes, including selection, development, deployment, oversight, monitoring and appropriate human resourcing.
- Licensed financial institutions should not employ AI models over which they have no control.
- AI use should be transparent and explainable, particularly for high-impact decisions.
- AI systems should be monitored, tested and reviewed continuously.
- Institutions remain responsible for outsourced AI functions and should secure contractual audit and information rights, data protection and cybersecurity provisions, performance guarantees and termination/cessation provisions.
- Institutions must retain the clear and immediate ability, with human intervention, to cease using an AI model, system or application.

**Interpretation for claims:** this guidance does not say that every insurance claim must always be decided manually. It does mean that the insurer must choose an oversight model proportionate to risk, retain accountability and provide meaningful human review for consequential decisions. Routine low-risk process steps may be automated under controls; an AI-generated recommendation is not automatically a binding claim decision.

### 14.4 What this means for insurer–TPA–AI relationships

The decision-authority analysis in Section 13 must now include a fourth actor: the AI system or model provider.

A practical responsibility map should distinguish:

| Layer | Possible role | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| Insurer | Owns policy, risk appetite, AI governance and customer/regulatory accountability | Policy, governance framework, CBUAE requirements, board accountability |
| TPA / claims administrator | Operates intake, workflow, assessment or delegated decisions within contract | Outsourcing agreement, delegated authority matrix, SLA |
