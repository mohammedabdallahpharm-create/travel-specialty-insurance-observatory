# UAE Travel & Pet Insurance — Claims Operations Research Dossier

**Research date:** 11 November 2026  
**Market:** United Arab Emirates  
**Scope:** Travel Insurance (including Baggage Protection) and companion-animal Pet Insurance  
**Research priority:** Claims Operations, role allocation, evidence requirements, decision authority and complaints  
**Status:** Initial desk research; not a comprehensive market census, legal opinion, or audit of actual insurer operations.

---

## 1. Executive findings

The public evidence reviewed supports several distinct operating models rather than one universal UAE claims model:

1. **AIG Travel Guard:** the policy identifies American Home Assurance Company (Dubai Branch) as insurer/underwriter and provides a direct claims department and portal. The policy language assigns claim notification, evidence requests and indemnity payments to the insurer ("We"). Public material does not identify a separate external TPA for this product.
2. **Allianz Travel UAE:** Alliance Insurance Company P.S.C. is identified as underwriter; NEXtCARE is identified as service provider. The public claims journey uses NEXtCARE email addresses and a dedicated claims assessor. The available public pages do not disclose the full delegation of authority or establish whether NEXtCARE can make final coverage decisions, approve reserves or authorise payments.
3. **ADNIC Travel:** ADNIC publishes a direct customer claims route and a documented sequence of incident reporting, document submission, assessment and claim-reference tracking. The assistance company is referred to as the company named in the policy schedule/certificate, not named in the reviewed claims page.
4. **MetLife Pet:** the policy wording identifies American Life Insurance Company (MetLife) as the insurer. MetLife names MedNet as its claims partner and uses MedNet's online reimbursement submission route. The public page says MetLife pays approved claims. This proves a claims submission partnership, not MedNet's final adjudication authority.
5. **MooPet:** policies issued before 1 June 2026 were underwritten by Alliance Insurance; policies issued on or after that date are underwritten by Qatar Insurance Company (QIC). The public process uses a form completed by the customer and veterinarian and an email address at kainosint.com. The public page does not identify Kainos' legal entity, contractual role or delegated authority.

**Central conclusion:** public-facing claims channels reveal who receives claims and supports the customer, but usually do not disclose the actual claims authority matrix. We can identify some insurer-level decision rights from policy wording, but cannot safely attribute delegated approval or rejection authority to TPAs, assistance providers or claims administrators without contractual evidence.

## 2. Evidence and source limitations

### Evidence classification used

- **Verified:** directly stated in a primary source for the specific claim.
- **Partially Verified:** the source establishes part of a role or process, but not the complete responsibility.
- **Unverified:** a plausible lead that lacks adequate corroboration.
- **Hypothesis:** an interpretation to be tested.
- **Historical:** evidence is valid for an earlier policy/product period and may not describe current terms.

### Source limitations

- Product marketing pages are useful for current customer journeys, but policy wording and certificates govern cover and contractual obligations.
- A published policy PDF may be an older version than the live product page. Version/date alignment must be checked before treating it as current.
- A named claims partner or portal proves a customer-facing service role; it does not by itself prove authority to accept, decline, settle or pay claims.
- Public information rarely exposes outsourcing contracts, delegated authority limits, audit rights, service-level agreements or internal escalation matrices.
- We have not interviewed the companies, inspected actual claim files, or independently tested any claims portal.

## 4. Travel Insurance — operating models

### 4.1 AIG Travel Guard

**Product / insurer**
- AIG states that Travel Guard is underwritten by American Home Assurance Company (Dubai Branch).
- The policy document identifies the Dubai Branch, its CBUAE authorisation and its claims department.

**Customer claims process**
- Customer notifies the insurer by telephone, email or online claims portal.
- The policy requires written notice within 90 days of the incident; it also sets a subsequent submission period. The precise interaction between these two periods should be interpreted from the full policy and certificate for an actual claim.
- The insurer furnishes claim forms/instructions and may request additional evidence.
- Policy wording states that indemnity payments are made by the insurer to the insured/appropriate party.
- AIG's current product page states that claims are processed within seven working days after all required documents are submitted.

**Evidence requirements and controls**
- AIG policy wording requires evidence of loss/expenses and may require further information.
- Police documentation is required for claims arising from criminal incidents.
- The policy includes fraud provisions allowing benefits to be forfeited and further action to be taken where dishonesty or exaggeration is established.
- The insurer reserves rights to obtain medical records and require medical examination in relevant cases.
- Baggage cover requires proof and official reporting in specified circumstances; unattended baggage and some carrier-custody situations are restricted.

**Decision authority**
- **Verified at insurer-contract level:** the policy consistently defines "We" as the insurer and assigns claim instructions, evidence requirements and payments to it.
- **Not established:** whether any third-party service provider performs intake, document indexing, medical review or preliminary assessment behind the scenes.
- **Research decision:** insurer-led public claims model; do not label it fully in-house until operational sourcing confirms that.

### 4.2 Allianz Travel UAE

**Product / role allocation**
- Allianz Travel UAE identifies Alliance Insurance Company P.S.C. as underwriter.
- It identifies NEXtCARE as service provider.
- Customer-facing claims and emergency contact channels use NEXtCARE domains and contact details.

**Customer claims process**
- Medical emergencies: 24/7 contact with the emergency assistance team; the published guidance says the team coordinates care and may arrange direct billing in participating/network hospitals.
- Non-emergency reimbursement: online claims portal, telephone, email or WhatsApp.
- A dedicated claims assessor reviews the case and contacts the claimant if additional information is needed.
- Approved claims are reimbursed according to policy terms.
- For baggage incidents, the published process requires an airline Property Irregularity Report (PIR) for airline-related loss/damage, proof of ownership, and police report for theft. It also gives product-specific reporting deadlines.
- For trip cancellation/interruption, it requires booking and cancellation evidence and proof of the covered reason.
- For medical claims, it asks for invoices, treatment reports, prescriptions and proof of travel.

**Decision authority**
- **Verified:** Alliance is the underwriter; NEXtCARE is the service provider; the dedicated assessor reviews claims.
- **Not established:** whether NEXtCARE has delegated authority to approve/decline claims or merely assesses and recommends decisions for Alliance.
- **Not established:** who has final authority for exceptional medical cases, high-value claims, fraud referrals, settlement overrides and appeals.
- **Research priority:** obtain the policy's claims procedure and complaint clauses, then seek direct confirmation of NEXtCARE's delegated authority.

### 4.3 ADNIC Travel

**Published customer journey**
1. Customer reports the incident and submits supporting documents.
2. ADNIC records incident details and assesses the case.
3. ADNIC provides next-step guidance and issues an SMS claim reference after the claim is opened.
4. The claimant can track progress using the reference.

**Visible evidence requirements**
- Baggage/flight claims: policy number, flight details, estimated value of lost items and any airline compensation.
- Travel document claims: details of the lost document, time/date, reporting authority and replacement cost.
- Accident/medical claims: incident details, police report for accidents, doctor/hospital details for illness.
- Emergency assistance: claimant is directed to the assistance company named in the schedule/certificate.

**Decision authority**
- **Verified:** ADNIC publicly describes itself as recording and assessing the travel claim.
- **Not established:** whether an external administrator or assistance company handles some stages.
- **Not established:** identity of assistance company across all ADNIC travel products; the schedule/certificate is product-specific.

### 4.4 Baggage Protection — analytical subdomain

Baggage claims are not simply "lost property reimbursement". The reviewed AIG and Allianz evidence shows a chain of evidence and possible recovery:

- Carrier/airline report (PIR) where loss or damage occurred in airline custody.
- Police report for theft.
- Proof of ownership and value: receipts, bank statements, warranty cards or photographs.
- Repair estimate and retention of damaged property where damage is repairable.
- Evidence of carrier compensation or other insurance payment.
- Time-bound notice/submission requirements.

**Operational implications**
- Claims assessors must distinguish loss, delay, damage and theft.
- The airline/handling agent is a source of evidence and potentially another compensating party, not automatically the insurer's claims administrator.
- Other insurance and carrier payments can affect the insured loss and recoverable amount.
- Proof-of-ownership gaps, missing PIRs and late police reports are predictable friction points.
- Subrogation and recovery rights may apply depending on policy terms.

**Research gap:** compare exact policy wording across AIG, Allianz and ADNIC before creating a common UAE baggage control framework. Do not treat Allianz's product-specific 21-day instruction as a market-wide rule.

## 5. Pet Insurance — operating models

### 5.1 MetLife Pet Insurance

**Insurer identity**
- The reviewed UAE policy wording names American Life Insurance Company (MetLife) as the company providing insurance.
- The policy is subject to UAE law and identifies the UAE branch.
- The live product page describes cover for dogs and cats, annual limits and reimbursement levels.

**Customer claims process**
1. Customer takes the pet to a registered veterinary clinic and pays the bill.
2. Customer submits an itemised veterinary invoice and receipt through MedNet's online reimbursement route.
3. MetLife confirms receipt and reviews the claim.
4. MetLife states that approved claims are reimbursed to the policyholder's bank account within seven days, subject to complete documentation.

**Policy controls visible in the wording**
- 30-day waiting period for illness; accident cover is described as commencing from the policy start in current product information.
- Pre-existing conditions and congenital/hereditary conditions are excluded in the reviewed policy wording.
- Treatment must be medically necessary and supported by evidence.
- Preventive care, grooming, elective/cosmetic procedures and other specified costs are excluded unless an endorsement changes the position.
- A UAE-licensed veterinarian is required in the policy wording.
- Deductible, covered percentage and maximum benefit shape the amount payable.
- The policy wording gives the insurer a right to obtain an independent expert opinion in a dispute about the correctness or appropriateness of a diagnosis.

**Important version discrepancy**
- The current MetLife product page says customers may use any registered veterinary clinic in the UAE and that there is no fixed vet network.
- The reviewed policy PDF defines a "Medical Network" as clinics/providers approved and published by MetLife, and the PDF includes wording that appears to restrict treatment outside that network.
- The PDF is an older published version. We cannot assume the current certificate/wording has the same network restriction or that the live page supersedes all policy terms.
- **Action:** obtain the current policy wording and certificate applicable to a new policy before making a definitive network statement.

**Decision authority**
- **Verified:** MedNet is the claims submission partner/channel; MetLife is the insurer and states that it pays approved claims.
- **Partially verified:** the policy grants the insurer a right to call for an independent expert opinion in diagnosis disputes.
- **Not established:** whether MedNet performs document checks only, medical/clinical review, coverage recommendations or delegated approval within financial limits.
- **Not established:** whether MedNet can reject claims or issue a binding decision on MetLife's behalf.

### 5.2 MooPet (MooCover)

**Underwriting transition**
- MooPet states that policies issued before 1 June 2026 were underwritten by Alliance Insurance.
- Policies issued on or after 1 June 2026 are underwritten by Qatar Insurance Company (QIC).
- The customer complaint channel changes accordingly: Alliance for the earlier cohort, QIC for the later cohort.

**Published claims process**
1. Customer downloads and completes the claim form.
2. A section of the form is completed by the veterinarian.
3. Customer emails the completed form and supporting information to claims.moopet@kainosint.com.
4. MooPet says covered claims are typically settled within ten business days after all information is received.
5. If more details are required, MooPet says it will contact the customer within five business days.

**Decision authority**
- **Verified:** underwriter identity depends on policy issue date.
- **Verified:** Kainos-branded email domain is the published claim-submission route.
- **Not established:** the legal identity of the entity behind the Kainos email, its role, its contract with MooPet/QIC, or its authority to approve/decline/settle claims.
- **Not established:** whether QIC handles claims directly or uses a delegated administrator.
- **Action:** identify the legal company behind Kainos International and search for policy wording, claims forms, privacy notice and corporate disclosures. Do not add Kainos to the verified entity registry until the role is corroborated.

## 7. Regulatory and complaint-resolution layer

### CBUAE

The Central Bank of the UAE regulates and supervises the insurance sector. It took over the former Insurance Authority's regulatory, supervisory, licensing and enforcement functions following the 2020 merger. This establishes the regulatory perimeter for licensed insurers; it does not make CBUAE the operational handler of an individual travel or pet claim.

### Sanadak

Sanadak is the independent ombudsman unit for eligible complaints involving CBUAE-licensed insurers and financial institutions.

The current Sanadak eligibility guidance says a complainant should:
- first submit an official complaint to the insurer;
- wait 15 calendar days after submitting it;
- not have the same complaint already before a court or be a duplicate; and
- meet the complainant/product eligibility criteria.

Sanadak may review the complaint and determine whether it is upheld, partially upheld or rejected. The published process may request additional information and can direct corrective action where a complaint is upheld.

**Important discrepancy:** MooPet's page says the customer may refer to Sanadak if dissatisfied or if no response is received within 30 days. Sanadak's current general eligibility page states 15 calendar days. We should use Sanadak's current official eligibility criteria for the general process and flag the MooPet page's 30-day statement as product-page wording requiring clarification, not silently harmonise the two.

### Implication for claims operations

The complaint layer is a separate control and accountability mechanism. A claims administrator may receive or assess a claim, but the consumer complaint route points to the licensed insurer and, if eligible, Sanadak. This makes it important to identify who owns the internal complaint response and who is accountable for correcting an adverse claim decision.

## 8. Key operational patterns and hypotheses

### Verified patterns
1. Travel emergency assistance and non-emergency reimbursement are operationally distinct.
2. Allianz's public model separates underwriting (Alliance) from customer-facing servicing/claims channels (NEXtCARE).
3. AIG's policy gives the insurer a direct claims department and explicit contractual claims/payment responsibilities.
4. ADNIC publishes a direct claims intake and assessment process.
5. MetLife uses a reimbursement model and a named external claims submission partner.
6. MooPet uses a veterinarian-supported claim form and an email submission process; the underwriter changed during 2026.
7. Evidence completeness is a visible control in all the reviewed claim journeys.
8. CBUAE supervision and Sanadak complaint escalation sit outside day-to-day claim intake.

### Hypotheses to test
- H1: Outsourced travel assistance/claims servicing can improve emergency coordination and customer access, while leaving contractual coverage accountability with the insurer.
- H2: Pet reimbursement models may reduce the need for direct billing networks but shift cash-flow and documentation burdens to policyholders.
- H3: The use of external claims partners may create an accountability gap if the customer cannot distinguish the submission channel from the decision owner.
- H4: Clinical documentation quality and policy exclusions are likely to be major sources of friction in pet claims, especially pre-existing conditions, waiting periods and medical necessity.
- H5: Baggage claims require multi-party evidence coordination across the insurer, airline/handler, police and sometimes other insurers.

These are not yet signals or market-wide findings. They are testable propositions derived from the small set of product examples.

## 9. Priority research questions still open

### Decision rights and delegation
- Does NEXtCARE have delegated authority to approve or decline Allianz claims? What are the monetary and claim-type limits?
- Who owns medical necessity review and escalation for Allianz emergency medical expenses?
- Does MedNet perform only digital intake/document checks, or also eligibility and clinical assessment for MetLife?
- Who signs off MetLife's claim decision and who handles complex veterinary disputes?
- Which legal entity receives, assesses and approves MooPet claims sent to Kainos?
- Does QIC retain all claim decisions or delegate authority to MooCover/Kainos?

### Claims controls
- What are the current product-specific notice and submission deadlines?
- What evidence is required for baggage delay, loss, damage and theft across each insurer?
- How are airline compensation and other insurance recoveries deducted?
- How are duplicate veterinary invoices, repeated treatment, pre-existing conditions and waiting-period claims detected?
- What is the process for independent veterinary review and appeal?

### Workforce and technology
- Which roles perform intake, document validation, medical/veterinary review, fraud checks, approval, payment and complaint resolution?
- Which tasks are performed by insurer staff, outsourced administrators, assistance providers or technology vendors?
- Are portals merely intake channels or do they include workflow automation, rules engines, OCR or AI?
- Who owns operational performance after a vendor platform or AI workflow goes live?
- What service-level metrics are publicly reported (turnaround time, first-time-right documentation, complaint rate, appeal overturn rate, leakage)?
