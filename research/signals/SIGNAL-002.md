- **Research decision:** Continue Research

## Executive assessment

Dubai Airports' 23 April 2024 recovery statement documented the operational impact of the exceptional rainfall event. It reported 2,155 cancelled flights, 115 diverted flights and more than 75,000 welfare packs distributed to disrupted guests. The statement also reported that normal operations had resumed by 23 April 2024.

These figures establish airport-level disruption and passenger support. They do not establish the number of affected travellers who held UAE-issued travel-insurance policies, the number or value of insurance claims, or the amount paid by insurers.

**Intelligence assessment:** this is the strongest UAE-local operational stress test among the three current records. Its insurance significance remains a research question, not a measured loss.

## Verified facts

- Dubai Airports reported 2,155 flight cancellations during the disruption.
- Dubai Airports reported 115 diverted flights.
- Dubai Airports reported distributing more than 75,000 welfare packs to disrupted guests.
- Dubai Airports stated that normal operations had resumed by 23 April 2024.

## Not established

- The number of affected passengers who held UAE-issued travel-insurance policies.
- The number or value of insurance claims arising from the event.
- Which individual expenses were met by airlines, airport support or insurers.
- The amount of any expense that remained unrecovered by an individual passenger.
- Whether any specific policy benefit was triggered for any individual traveller.

## Link to the existing observatory structure

### Domain and coverage lens
- **Domain:** Travel Insurance.
- **Subdomain:** no single subdomain is assigned to the airport-wide event because the source reports multiple outcomes.
- At passenger level, classify the actual outcome separately: cancellation, delay, diversion, missed connection or trip interruption. Do not collapse them into one category.

### Entity Registry
- Dubai Airports is the primary source and airport operator for the reported operational figures.
- No insurer, TPA or assistance provider is linked to an actual claim from this event in the current evidence.
- Do not infer a claims-administration relationship from airport passenger-support activity.

### Policy Intelligence
- **POLICY-A:** the existing comparison contains a provisional delay trigger and selected covered-cause observations. The complete wording, schedule and relevant 2024 version need revalidation.
- **POLICY-B:** the existing comparison contains a provisional delay trigger and selected covered-cause / authority-action observations. Exact version and terms need revalidation.
- The current comparison is not sufficient to determine cancellation, interruption, delay, diversion or additional-expense eligibility.
- Weather as an event description does not, by itself, prove that every passenger loss is covered.

### Claims Operations
For an individual case, the file should establish:
1. Booking, ticket, itinerary and passenger travel dates.
2. Carrier confirmation of cancellation, delay or diversion and the stated reason.
3. Actual departure / arrival times and any missed connection.
4. Rebooking, refund and passenger-care support offered or provided by the airline or other parties.
5. Accommodation, meals, transport and rearrangement receipts.
6. Any airline refund, compensation or other recovery.
7. The applicable policy schedule, wording, definitions, exclusions and endorsements.

The claims assessment must distinguish the airport-wide event record from the passenger's own itinerary and unrecovered loss.

## Competing hypotheses — not findings

- Some affected travellers may have incurred additional accommodation, transport or rearrangement expenses.
- Some passenger needs may have been met through airline, airport or other support rather than insurance.
- Some passengers may have had no unrecovered expense or may not have held travel insurance.

These hypotheses require passenger-level or aggregate claims evidence; the airport recovery statement does not resolve them.

## Operational and commercial implications

- A prolonged airport disruption can create a surge in customer contacts, rebooking questions, evidence collection and claims triage.
- Claims and assistance teams need a consistent distinction between cancellation, delay, diversion and missed connection.
- Airport statistics can corroborate the event timeline but cannot substitute for claimant-specific proof.
- No public claims dataset is currently linked to this event; claims frequency, paid loss and insurer exposure must remain unknown.

## Evidence gaps and next research

- Review applicable UAE passenger-welfare and airline-care requirements during the April 2024 disruption.
- Identify official airline notices and the actual support or recovery available to affected passengers.
- Revalidate complete, version-matched POLICY-A and POLICY-B wordings, including cancellation, interruption, delay, exclusions and recovery provisions.
- Search for public aggregate travel-insurance claims or assistance disclosures directly linked to this event.

**Next research question:** What passenger-care and recovery arrangements applied during the April 2024 Dubai disruption, and what individual expenses, if any, remained unrecovered and potentially relevant under the applicable policy wording?
