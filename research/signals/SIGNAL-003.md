# SIGNAL-003 — UAE-002: GCAA temporarily suspends ADS-B OUT mandate amid GPS interference, June 2025

- **Domain:** Travel Insurance
- **Subdomain:** Not assigned; the regulatory decision is not itself a passenger insurance benefit event
- **Event / decision date:** 26 June 2025
- **Publication / observation:** 26 June 2025 / 15 November 2026
- **Market:** United Arab Emirates
- **Signal type:** Aviation safety / navigation and surveillance-system resilience
- **Evidence status:** Partially Verified
- **Confidence:** High for the regulatory decision; passenger-facing impact not established
- **Research decision:** Continue Research
- **Record status:** Under review

## Executive assessment

The UAE General Civil Aviation Authority issued Safety Decision 2025-02 on 26 June 2025, temporarily suspending the mandatory ADS-B OUT requirement for specified operations within UAE airspace. The decision cited increased GPS interference / jamming affecting ADS-B OUT reliability and directed operators to use alternative approved surveillance means and comply with ATC instructions.

This is a technical safety mitigation and regulatory response. The decision itself does not establish that a passenger flight was cancelled, delayed or diverted, or that a travel-insurance claim or loss occurred.

**Intelligence assessment:** this is a UAE-specific aviation-resilience signal. Its connection to passenger travel and insurance remains conditional on evidence of an actual operational consequence.

## Verified facts

- The GCAA decision is dated 26 June 2025 and applies to specified IFR operations within UAE controlled airspace and the Emirates FIR, and to air navigation service providers.
- The GCAA cited increased GPS interference / jamming as adversely affecting ADS-B OUT reliability.
- The decision temporarily suspended specified ADS-B OUT requirements and directed reliance on alternative approved surveillance means.

## Not established

- That the decision caused a specific passenger flight cancellation, delay, diversion or route change.
- That the measure represented a complete closure of UAE airspace.
- That a UAE travel-insurance claim arose from the decision.
- That any passenger incurred an insured or unrecovered expense.
- That the regulatory mitigation resulted in a measurable insurance-market loss.

### Claims Operations
Before opening an insurance-exposure assessment, establish:
1. The relevant GCAA notice, NOTAM or air navigation instruction and its effective period.
2. The operator / airline response and any actual route or schedule change.
3. The passenger's booking, itinerary, scheduled and actual flight details.
4. The carrier-confirmed reason for any cancellation, delay or diversion.
5. Any additional expense and support or recovery received.
6. The applicable policy wording and schedule, if a passenger-level loss is evidenced.

A technical notice alone is contextual evidence; it is not a substitute for proof of interruption to the insured journey.
